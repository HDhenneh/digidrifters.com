Federal Presidential Republic · Updated October 2026

The United States has the world's most imitated constitutional architecture — separation of powers, an independent judiciary, a free press, and regular elections. The second Trump term has tested how far that architecture bends: aggressive executive orders, an extra-constitutional efficiency body (DOGE), sweeping tariffs, and a Supreme Court immunity ruling that widened presidential latitude, met by courts that have blocked dozens of those orders. But formal structures only partly describe who holds power. A parallel architecture — K Street lobbying, unlimited Super PAC spending since Citizens United, a permanent national-security bureaucracy, and a financial-and-tech complex managing trillions — shapes policy regardless of who wins elections.
